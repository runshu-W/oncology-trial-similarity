\section{Configuration constants and reproducibility details}

\paragraph{Candidate features (9 dimensions).} As defined in the main text:
$s_i$ = Stage-2 overall similarity $/100$; disease, regimen, endpoint,
follow-up, and eligibility match scores = the corresponding Stage-2 $0$--$5$
dimension scores $/5$; result-quality score $/5$; negative red-flag severity
$-\mathrm{rf}_i \in [-1,0]$; and $\log(1+n_i)$. Stage-2 dimension scores are
produced by the deterministic reranker released with the code
(\texttt{pipeline/\allowbreak oncology\_trial\_similarity\_pipeline.py}); the scoring
rules, keyword tables, and gate conditions are implemented there verbatim and
exercised by the released unit tests.

\paragraph{Gate and rule prior.} The conservative gate multiplies factors for
endpoint compatibility ($<1.5/5$ blocks), result usability ($0$ blocks),
disease match ($<1.5/5$ scales by $0.2$; $<2.5/5$ by $0.6$), and low-severity
red flags (scale $0.5$). Rule discounts $a^{\mathrm{rule}}_i$ follow the
suitability ladder $0.75/0.40/0.15/0$; the rule prior fixes $\lambda_0=0.2$
with a flat $\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)$ anchor.

\paragraph{Selective model.} DeepSets encoder $\phi$: $9 \to 16 \to 16$
(ReLU); score head $\rho$ and discount head: $32 \to 16 \to 1$; weak-component
head $\psi$: $16 \to 16 \to 1$ on the pooled context. Training: Adam, learning
rate $0.01$, $100$ epochs, full batch, seed $20260603$ (primary and every
window refit); KL-to-rule weight $\rho = 0.1$ on normalised candidate
allocation; listwise term disabled ($\eta = 0$); borrowed-information penalty
$10^{-4}\max(\sum_i \lambda_i a_i n_i - 100, 0)^2$. EB anchors by
beta-binomial MLE (grid plus coordinate refinement): training split
$\mathrm{Beta}(0.68, 1.70)$; past-only fits $\mathrm{Beta}(0.65,1.61)$,
$(0.68,1.70)$, $(0.69,1.72)$ at the 2020/2021/2022-12-31 cutoffs.
Disease-stratified EB: per-primary-site MLE for strata with $\ge 40$ training
queries, global fallback otherwise.

\paragraph{Evaluation constants.} Held-out split: deterministic $80/20$
(seed $20260603$), $n = 281$. Disjoint forward windows by primary completion
date: (2020-12-31, 2021-12-31] $n=181$; (2021-12-31, 2022-12-31] $n=204$;
after 2022-12-31, $n=390$; past-only training pools $632/813/1{,}017$.
Paired bootstrap: $4{,}000$ resamples, percentile intervals. PIT: randomized
form, RNG seed $20260707$; prediction intervals: equal-tailed on the exact
beta-binomial mixture pmf. Pre-specified information subgroup: $I = \sum_i
\mathrm{gate}_i a^{\mathrm{rule}}_i n_i \ge 8$, fixed from exploratory
analysis of the two earlier windows before the final window was examined.
Robust-MAP baseline: DerSimonian--Laird heterogeneity on the rate scale,
Haldane-smoothed rates, MAP ESS cap $200$, $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$,
robustification weights $w \in \{0.5, 0.9\}$. SAM adapter: temperature
$\tau = 1$; multiplier $\min(1, R^{\tau})$ with $R$ the
historical-to-weak predictive ratio at the observed outcome.

\paragraph{Gold-standard simulation.} As released in
\texttt{simulation/gold\_standard/}: $2{,}000$ replicates per cell (estimation
and design worlds), simulation training set $300$ queries, $60$ epochs, seed
$20260720$; design test $H_0: \theta \le 0.20$ at one-sided $0.025$,
alternative $\theta_0 = 0.35$; conflict grid $0$--$1.5$ logit shift; success
threshold $0.975$; fixed-discount comparator $0.35$. Data-generating
constants, trap/hidden-gem composition, and per-cell outputs are archived in
\texttt{results/tables/\allowbreak gold\_standard\_*}.

\paragraph{Robust-MAP tuning detail.} The tuned robust
meta-analytic-predictive (MAP) prior of Table~3 of the main text is a
robustified mixture of a pooled MAP component and a vague component in the
style of the RBesT tools. Its best-tuned configuration reached test NLL
$3.043$ --- better than flat-anchored borrowing but above the plain EB
reference --- and is sensitive to its robustification weight, degrading to
$3.198$ at $w = 0.9$ as a single pooled MAP over-borrows from
homogeneously treated historical evidence.

\paragraph{Second worked case study (withholding limits harm).}
NCT03046953 is a completed single-arm Phase~2 trial of avelumab in
relapsed or refractory peripheral T-cell lymphoma, with its objective
response held out (true outcome $5$ of $32$, observed ORR $0.156$; a

\section{The prospective conflict signal: full analysis (moved from the
main text, round 6)}

The ablation gave a clear and initially unwelcome answer. Adding the
prospective set-level conflict signal changed the borrowing-discrimination
ROC-AUC (primary, parameter-level labels) by $+0.017$, $+0.008$, $+0.006$,
$+0.001$ and $+0.005$ across the five scenarios for the fixed-budget
architecture, and by $+0.012$ to $-0.000$ for the selective architecture
--- negligible relative to Monte Carlo error --- and did not improve type
I error. On the outcome the signal was designed to improve, it does
essentially nothing.

The reason is structural. The signal measures a donor's deviation from
the consensus of the remaining donors, so it can only detect
heterogeneity \emph{within} the candidate set. When prior-data conflict
displaces the whole set uniformly, the consensus moves with it and no
donor appears deviant. Under conflict the ROC-AUC of every design-time
method fell below chance ($0.43$--$0.45$ in scenario S4 under the primary
labels; the selective prior itself scored $0.429$), because surface
features actively mislead when the entire retrieved history is biased.
Detecting that situation requires the current outcome: UIP-JS, which
computes its weights against the current data, attained $0.813$ under the
primary labels ($0.839$ under the process-compatible labels) in the same
scenario.

The signal does, however, do something else in the fixed-budget
architecture, which the borrowed-size response curve
(Figure~\ref{fig:s4-ess}) makes visible. Without it, the learned discount
head is inert: the per-source borrowed sample size is flat across donors
at every true distance from the query rate ($43.6$, $43.7$, $43.8$,
$43.9$ across distance bins, pooled over the five scenarios). With the
signal, the borrowed size declines monotonically with true distance
($31.3$, $30.4$, $28.0$, $23.6$). The nine reranking dimensions describe
design similarity only; none carries information about outcome-space
discrepancy, so a discount head conditioned on them alone collapses to a
constant, and the set-level signal supplies exactly the missing
information while never touching the query outcome.

The selective architecture changes this picture in a way we did not
anticipate and report as a finding. Even with the prospective signal, the
selective model's discount response is flat ($43.6$, $43.7$, $43.8$,
$43.9$): once the total-borrowing head exists, training reallocates the
comparability response to the set level and the per-source discount stops
grading donors (Figure~\ref{fig:s4-ess}). Per-donor discrimination under
the selective model is therefore no better than under the fixed-budget
model (Figure~\ref{fig:s4-roc}) --- its gains come from deciding how much
to borrow overall, not from grading whom to borrow from more finely. The
defensible claim is narrower than the one we set out to make: the
prospective signal does not improve donor ranking; in the fixed-budget
model it is what makes the per-source discount function at all; and in
the selective model even that role disappears.
